Engineering take-home

Teach kids
to sum coins

Use Claude Code to build and host a small web app that teaches kids to find the sum of coins: map the skill as a mini knowledge graph, teach one lesson from it, and build a multiplayer practice game.

The goal

A kid who can total a handful of coins

By the end, a K-2 kid can look at a handful of coins (pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters) and say what it's worth. Dollar bills are excluded from this exercise.

Start from zero

Assume the student knows nothing: no numbers, no counting, no addition, no idea what a coin is. They can read, so leave reading and English out of scope.

The capstone sits around CCSS 2.MD.C.8; its prerequisites reach back through kindergarten counting and grade 1 addition.

Part 1

The knowledge graph

Before you can teach the skill, map what it's made of. Build a mini knowledge graph from zero to "find the sum of a handful of coins":

  • Each node is a knowledge component: one small, teachable piece (for example, "count objects to 10" or "know a dime is worth 10¢").
  • Edges are prerequisites: a KC depends only on KCs below it.
  • Pitch every KC for a K-2 kid: tiny steps, concrete before abstract.
  • Roughly 10 to 15 nodes is plenty.

Ship the graph in the repo as a diagram or structured data, whichever is clearest.

Part 2

The lesson

Choose one KC from your graph and build one complete lesson that teaches it. Assume the student has mastered its prerequisites and nothing else.

The lesson should be direct instruction and narrated (free TTS is fine).

End the lesson with 10 problems that test mastery of the lesson. If the student fails, detect what they got wrong and where they need help in this lesson. You do not need to implement the remediation itself, just the detection.

No multiple choice. All answers are typed responses. Assume the kid knows how to type.

Part 3

Multiplayer practice

A multiplayer game for summing coins, the top of your graph. It unlocks when the lesson is complete.

Two or more students: racing your friends at a cash register, working together at an amusement park to handle customers, whatever you like. A minimal backend or hosted realtime service is fine.

Priorities: the graph, the lesson, and the multiplayer game are the core. Get all three working end to end before polishing anything else. Gamification is a plus, not a requirement.

Submission

Send back an email containing

  • The hosted URL, playable start to finish
  • The repo with full git history, including your knowledge graph
  • LEARNING.pdf: explains the knowledge graph, the KC breakdowns, and the lesson flow plus remediation
  • FRONTEND.pdf: explains your frontend and gamification work (asset generation, UI development, architecture), showing not just that you can make a game but that you build proper frontend architecture
  • MULTIPLAYER.pdf: explains the technical features of the backend and the multiplayer
  • The estimated time it took you, from start to deliverable

What we look for

  • The graph and the lesson: your understanding of learning science, and a frontend that flows without logic errors.
  • The multiplayer: it actually works, and you can explain how it works when we walk through it.
