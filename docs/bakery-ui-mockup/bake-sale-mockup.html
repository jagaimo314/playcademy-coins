Bake Sale — art direction

Every colour below was sampled out of the supplied reference sheet, not chosen. The rule that follows from it is one line long: the food is warm, the machinery is cool, and one ink outlines both. That is what makes a tray readable at a glance against a belt, and a belt readable against the wall behind it.

Food palette sampled from the reference

Chrome palette the room the food travels through

Rules

  1. One ink, and it is warm. #201611, sampled off the reference's own outlines — not black, and not the blue-black the app currently inks with. Outlines scale with the object: 3px on a tray or a duct, 2.5px on a coin or a meter, never a hairline. Shadows are the same ink at 90%, offset, never blurred.
  2. Warmth is depth. The back room is cool and desaturated, the counter is warm wood, the players' panels are white. A child reads three planes without a single perspective line, and the food — the warmest thing on screen — sits on top of all of it.
  3. The price is the loudest thing on a tray. It is the whole mechanic, so it gets the display face at 22px, near-black on warm white, on a card affixed to the front of the deck. A price that floats free of its tray reads as a separate object.
  4. Nothing means one thing by colour alone. The two meters differ by label, by position and by direction as well as by hue. The two duct ends differ by a glow, not just by a fill. Your own panel is lifted and outlined and says “(you)”.
  5. Count, don't estimate. The meters are ten cells, not a smooth bar. A K–2 kid can count cells; nobody can read a bar that is 30% along.
  6. No gradients, no blur, no texture. Flat fills and hard edges throughout, which is what the reference does and what keeps the goods legible at 76px.

What is drawn how

Baked goodsSix cutouts, background flood-filled to transparent and embedded as PNG data URIs. The cake and the brownie are the revised references; the other four still come off the original sheet.
Everything elseHTML and CSS. Border-radius, borders, hard box-shadows, gradients only for the belt's slot dividers. No SVG.
CoinsThe one exception. The faces are the real paths out of components/coin/coin-faces.js, and the discs keep the component's real diameter ratios — quarter 1, nickel 0.87, penny 0.78, dime 0.73 — so a hand reads like a hand.

Open questions

  • The cutouts are placeholders. The four originals come off a watermarked stock comp. Licence them or redraw the set before this ships — and the set needs to grow: cookies, bread and flan are named in the brief and are not in this sheet.
  • Six goods, twenty-seven trays. At this density the same macaron appears three times on one belt. Either the set grows to twelve or the goods stop carrying identity and the price does all the work.
  • The coin faces bottom out at about 56px — and only after darkening the metals. The shipped *-deep shades (#AF6A3B, #A2AEBF) sit too close to their own metal to hold a portrait at panel size; this frame uses #92511F and #808FA3. Either the shades deepen or the panel sets a floor under --pc-coin-size.
  • The ink change is not free. Moving to a warm ink means --pc-ink changes for the Lesson and the Home screen too.
